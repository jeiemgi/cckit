**cckit does not publish.** An approved piece is part of the project's shared version and it
builds, and that is where cckit stops. Putting it on a public address is done by the technical
person or by your hosting service, the way your site is put online today. What you do is hand them
the right information. The example is the Easter menu page of the Bakery site, a neighbourhood
bakery's website.

**Who does it:** you check that the piece is done and hand it over. The technical person or the
hosting service puts it online. Your assistant writes the note and does not send it.

**What you will have at the end:** a message to the person who publishes, with everything they
need, and a piece you have seen at its public address.

<Steps>

1. **Check that the piece is done.** Only an approved and finished piece is ready to hand over.

   <Chat
     title="checking that the Easter menu page is done"
     say="Where are we? Is the Easter menu page done?"
     means="Done means approved and part of the shared version. A piece in review is not ready."
   >
     Yes. The Easter menu page is done: it was approved and it is part of the shared version.
   </Chat>

2. **Ask for the note to the person who publishes.** Say when it should be online.

   <Chat
     title="writing the note for whoever puts the Easter menu page online"
     say="Write a note for the technical person: the Easter menu page is approved and ready to go online. Say which page it is, what changed and that I want it online by Thursday."
     means="Your assistant writes the note. You send it. Nothing is published by writing it."
   >
     Here is the note. "The Easter menu page is approved and finished. It is the page 'Easter menu'
     on the Bakery site. It adds the Easter loaf and its price and the ordering deadline. Please put
     it online by Thursday and tell me when it is live." I have not sent it. I cannot put anything
     online.
   </Chat>

3. **Send the note and the review link.** Send it the way you usually write to that person: email,
   chat or a message. Add the link of the approved review, which shows exactly what was approved.

4. **Look at the page at its public address.** When the technical person says it is live, open
   the address yourself and read the page once, on a phone too. If something is off, it is a
   change: see [Update a page that already exists](/marketing/update-a-page/).

</Steps>

## What to hand over

| Give them | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The name of the piece and the page it belongs to | So they publish the right one. |
| The link of the approved review | It shows exactly what was approved. |
| The day and time it should be online | Some pieces have a date, such as an Easter menu. |
| What to check once it is live | So you both know when it is done. |

<Aside type="note" title="What cckit does not do here">
It does not publish a page, send an email, schedule a post or measure how a piece performed. Those
stay in the tools your team already uses, as in
[after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval). Whether your site goes online by
itself when a piece is approved depends on how it is hosted, and cckit does not know that.
</Aside>

<Outcomes title="For the technical person: what you are being handed">

The approved piece is on the project's shared version (its main line on GitHub) and the project's
check passed before the review opened. Publishing is outside cckit: do it the way this site is
normally put online. Background on merging: [Why merging is a decision](/ship/).

</Outcomes>
