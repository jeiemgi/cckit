A brand voice that lives in someone's head has to be explained again in every conversation. With
the marketing profile you write it down once, in the project. Claude reads it before it writes or
plans anything.

## What gets recorded

| Part | What it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Voice** | How do we sound? | "Direct and warm. Short sentences. We say 'you', never 'users'." |
| **Key messages** | What do we always want the reader to take away? | "Set up in a day. No contract." |
| **Banned phrasings** | What do we never say? | "Revolutionary", "best-in-class", exclamation marks in headlines. |

## Set it

<Steps>

1. **Start the conversation.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Help me set our brand voice for this project. Ask me what you need to know, one question at a time.
   ```

   </div>

2. **Answer with examples.** Paste two or three pieces you think sound right, and one that sounds
   wrong. Examples tell Claude more than adjectives do.

3. **Read what Claude wrote back.** It shows you the voice, the key messages and the banned
   phrasings before it saves them. Correct anything that is off.

4. **Send it for review.** The brand voice is content like any other: it goes through a draft and
   a review, so the team agrees on it before it applies to everyone.

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Save the brand voice and ask for a review.
   ```

   </div>

</Steps>

Once it is approved, every draft and every campaign plan in this project starts from it.

## Check that a draft follows it

<div class="cckit-prompt">

```text title="Say this to Claude"
Check this draft against our brand voice. List every sentence that breaks it and propose a fix for each.
```

</div>

## Change it later

The voice changes the same way it was set: say what should change, read the result, ask for a
review. The history of every change stays in the project, with who approved it.

<Aside type="tip">
Keep the banned list short and specific. "No jargon" is hard to check. "Never say 'leverage'" is easy.
</Aside>
