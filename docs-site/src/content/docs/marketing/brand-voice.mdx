Without a written voice, every draft comes back sounding like an advert and you correct the same
thing each time. The voice has to be explained to your assistant again in every conversation, and
every handed-over piece is corrected by whoever remembers it. Written once, every piece starts in
the right voice. Pilot Light Bakery's project is new, and its voice is the first thing written
into it.

**Who does it:** the team decides the voice. Your assistant asks the questions and writes the
answers down, and the editor assistant checks drafts against them.

**What you will have at the end:** one approved file in the project, `brand/voice.md`, that your
assistant reads before it writes a draft or plans a campaign.

This is the written voice only. Logo, color and visual identity are not part of it.

## What gets recorded

| Part | What it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Voice** | How do we sound? | "Warm and plain. Short sentences. We say 'you'." As in: "Come in, the bread is still warm." |
| **Key messages** | What do we always want the reader to take away? | "Baked fresh every morning: the ovens start at 5." |
| **Banned phrasings** | What do we never say? | "Artisanal", "excellence", exclamation marks in headlines. |

## Set it

<Steps>

1. **Start the conversation.**

   <Chat
     title="starting the brand voice for the bakery"
     say="Help me set our brand voice for this project. Ask me what you need to know, one question at a time."
     means="Your assistant reads the project first and asks only what the project does not answer."
   >
     Yes. The project is new, so I have nothing to read yet. My first question: when a neighbour
     walks in, how do you talk to them at the counter?
   </Chat>

2. **Answer with examples.** Paste two or three pieces you think sound right, and one that sounds
   wrong. Examples tell your assistant more than adjectives do.

   <Chat
     title="giving examples of the bakery voice"
     say="These sound like us: “Come in, the bread is still warm.” and “Order by Thursday, collect on Saturday.” This one does not: “Experience our artisanal baking excellence!”"
     means="One wrong example is as useful as the right ones."
   >
     Thank you. I hear short sentences, an invitation and no hype. One more question: do we say
     "you" to the customer, or do we talk about "our guests"?
   </Chat>

3. **Read what your assistant wrote back.** It shows you the voice, the key messages and the
   banned phrasings before it saves them. Correct anything that is off. A key message needs its
   proof: a number, a customer, a fact. One without proof is recorded as unproven, and your
   assistant does not state it as fact in a draft.

   <Chat
     title="reading the bakery voice before it is saved"
     say="Show me what you have written down before you save it."
     means="Nothing is saved until you say so. Correct what is off."
   >
     Voice: warm and plain, short sentences, we say "you". Key message: "Baked fresh every morning",
     with its proof, that the ovens start at 5. Recorded as unproven: "Best bread in the
     neighbourhood", because there is no proof for it yet, so I will not state it as a fact in a
     draft. Banned: "artisanal", "excellence" and exclamation marks in headlines.
   </Chat>

4. **Send it for review.** The brand voice is content like any other: it goes through a draft and
   a review, so the team agrees on it before it applies to everyone.

   <Chat
     title="sending the bakery voice for review"
     say="Save the brand voice and ask for a review."
     means="Once it is approved, every draft and every campaign plan in this project starts from it."
   >
     Saved as brand/voice.md in a draft of its own, and the review is open. Here is the link for
     your colleague.
   </Chat>

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

Open a new conversation and ask for a draft without describing the voice. If the draft sounds
right and avoids the banned phrasings, the voice is in place.

<Chat
  title="checking that the voice is in place"
  say="Write two lines announcing the Easter menu."
  means="You did not describe the voice, so the draft came from the file."
>
  Easter loaves are in the oven. Order by Thursday, collect on Saturday. That follows the voice:
  short sentences, "you" and none of the banned words.
</Chat>

To check one draft against it:

<Chat
  title="checking a draft against the bakery voice"
  say="Check this draft against our brand voice. List every sentence that breaks it and propose a fix for each."
  means="The editor assistant does the check. You decide which fixes to keep."
>
  Two sentences break the voice. "Experience our artisanal baking" uses a banned word, so I
  propose "Come in and smell the bread". The exclamation mark in the headline is banned, so I
  propose a full stop.
</Chat>

## Change it later

The voice changes the same way it was set: say what should change, read the result, ask for a
review. The history of every change stays in the project, with who approved it.

<Aside type="tip">
Keep the banned list short and specific. "No hype" is hard to check. "Never say 'artisanal'" is easy.
</Aside>
