<TutorialMeta />

A piece is rarely right the first time. The price is off, the tagline is too long, a reviewer
wants a line moved. You say what to change in plain words and your assistant changes it. The
example is the Easter menu page of the Bakery site, a neighbourhood bakery's website, while its
review is open.

**Who does it:** you say what to change. Your assistant makes the change and updates the review.
A person other than you approves.

**What you will have at the end:** the same review, updated, and a reviewer who can read it again.

<Steps>

1. **Say what to change.** Say which piece and what you want, as you would to a colleague.

   <Chat
     title="changing the price and the deadline on the Easter menu page"
     say="On the Easter menu page, change the Easter loaf price to 6 and move the ordering deadline to Friday. Update the review."
     means="You do not edit anything. The review link stays the same."
   >
     Done. The Easter loaf is now 6 and the ordering deadline is Friday. I updated the review, and
     the link is the one you already sent.
   </Chat>

2. **Look before you change.** When you are not sure, ask for options first. Nothing changes until
   you pick one.

   <Chat
     title="asking for other taglines for the Easter menu page"
     say="Show me two other taglines for the Easter menu page. Do not change anything yet."
     means="Asking for options changes nothing. Say which one you want when you are ready."
   >
     Two options: "Baked this morning, on the table for Easter" and "Easter bread, still warm".
     The page keeps its current tagline until you choose.
   </Chat>

3. **Hand over a reviewer's comments.** When the approver asked for changes, your assistant reads
   their comments and applies them. It tells you which ones it did not apply.

   <Chat
     title="applying the reviewer's comments on the Easter menu page"
     say="Read the review comments on the Easter menu page and apply them. Tell me which ones you did not apply and why."
     means="You decide what to change. Your assistant reports every comment it left alone."
   >
     I applied the comment about the opening line and the one about the price. I left one alone: it
     asks for a photo of the Easter loaf, and there is no photo in the project. Send me one and I
     will add it.
   </Chat>

4. **Tell the approver.** Send them a short message that the same link is updated. The page
   refreshes by itself and the approver reads it again. This repeats until the answer is
   **Approve**. After that, [finish the piece](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="A finished piece is closed">
Once a piece is approved and finished, it is closed. A change to it is a new piece with its own
review: see [Update a page that already exists](/marketing/update-a-page/).
</Aside>

<Outcomes title="For the technical person: what runs">

<div data-table="grid">

| What you say | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| "Update the review." | `cckit task finish <id> "<summary>" --all` |
| Work on the piece again | `cckit task start <id>` |

</div>

`cckit task finish` commits the tracked changes, pushes them and, when a review is already open,
reports it instead of creating a second one. `--all` also adds new files. `cckit task start`
reuses the draft's folder when it exists and refuses a task that is done. `cckit task run` is not
used here: a task that is already in review starts no agent. Reading the reviewer's comments and
editing the draft are done by the assistant itself, not by a `cckit` verb.

</Outcomes>
