<TutorialMeta />

Handing a piece over is right for a page. A post you care about sentence by sentence is better
written together: you and your assistant work in a draft of your own, and the shared version does
not change until the piece is approved. On the Bakery site the landing page is approved; now the
owner of Pilot Light Bakery wants a post about the new rye loaf.

**Who does it:** you decide what the post says. Your assistant and its editor helper propose and
write. Your colleague approves.

**What you will have at the end:** the post as part of the shared version, and the piece marked
done.

<Steps>

1. **Say what you want to make.**

   <Chat
     title="starting a post about the new rye loaf"
     say="I want to write a post about our new seeded rye loaf. Note it as a new piece and let's start the draft."
     means="The draft is yours alone. The shared version of the site does not change."
   >
     I added it to the task list and started the draft. Who should read the post, and what do you
     want them to do after reading it?
   </Chat>

2. **Agree on an outline.**

   <Chat
     title="agreeing on an outline for the rye loaf post"
     say="The reader is a neighbour who has not tried our bread. I want them to come in on Saturday and try the rye. Propose an outline."
     means="Change the outline until it is right: what to cut, what to move, what is missing."
   >
     Here is an outline: a short opening about the rye, what goes in it, when it comes out of the
     oven on Saturday, and one line inviting the reader in. Do you want the story of the recipe
     before the invitation?
   </Chat>

3. **Write the draft.**

   <Chat
     title="writing the first draft of the rye loaf post"
     say="No story, keep it short. Write the first draft from that outline, in our brand voice."
     means="Edit it together: “make the opening shorter”, “this paragraph sounds like an ad”, “add the price”."
   >
     The first draft is written, without the recipe story. It follows the brand voice your team
     set: short sentences, "you", no hype. Here it is.
   </Chat>

   If your team has [set its brand voice](/marketing/brand-voice/), your assistant reads it first.

4. **Ask for a review.**

   <Chat
     title="asking for a review of the rye loaf post"
     say="This is ready. Save it and ask for a review."
     means="Your assistant saves the draft, sends it to GitHub and gives you a link."
   >
     I saved the draft and it is ready for review. Here is the link to send to your colleague.
   </Chat>

5. **Your colleague reviews it, and you apply the changes.** Send the link to the person who
   approves. GitHub does not let the person who asked for the review approve it, so the approver
   is someone else; if you work alone, see
   [If you work alone](/marketing/review-and-approval/#if-you-work-alone). When comments come back,
   hand them to your assistant as on
   [Ask for changes to a piece](/marketing/ask-for-changes/).

6. **Approved.** Once it is approved, tell your assistant so, and it finishes the piece (see
   [After approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval)).

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

Ask "Where are we?". The post is no longer listed as in progress or waiting for a review, and it
counts as done.
