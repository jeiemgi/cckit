You do not follow this page. It tells you what happens once you describe a piece, so that the
link you get back is not a surprise.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. **The piece is recorded.** Your description becomes an entry on the task list the project keeps,
   with a title and a short description. What is not on the list is not being worked on.
2. **It is built apart from the approved version.** The approved version of the project holds only
   what has been approved. The piece is built in its own draft, a separate copy, so a failed
   attempt cannot change approved content.
3. **It is checked.** Before anything is shown to you, the project's own check runs. For a
   website project that check is a build plus a lint, and it passed before the review link was
   opened. When the check fails, or when nothing changed, no review is opened and the run says
   why.
4. **A review is opened.** You get one link. The draft is read there, commented on there and
   answered there.
5. **A person approves.** A review ends in "approved" or "changes needed", said by a person.
   Claude never approves. Once it is approved, the piece is part of the approved version and is
   marked done.

A campaign is the same process for several pieces under one plan. See
[Several pieces, one goal](/marketing/campaigns/).

## What stays with you

| Moment | Why it is yours |
| --- | --- |
| Describing what you want | Only you know what the piece is for. |
| Answering a question | When you work with Claude in a conversation, it asks before it guesses. A handed-over task does not stop to ask: it picks a reading and the review shows it. |
| Approving | The approver is a person other than whoever asked for the work. |
| Publishing | cckit does not publish. That step is outside it. |

## Assistants prepare, people decide

Claude brings four assistants to the project: a planner, an editor, a designer and a researcher.
They propose plans, write drafts, check facts and apply review comments. They do not decide what
gets made, what a draft finally says or whether a piece is approved. The full list is on
[Who does what](/marketing/who-does-what/).

## What the process does not cover

- **Publishing.** The process ends at an approved version. Putting it on your site, sending the
  email and scheduling the post happen in the tools you already use. See
  [after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).
- **Results.** cckit has no analytics. A campaign plan has a place for the measured result, and
  your team supplies the number.
