title: "How we work"
description: "The method in plain words: every piece is planned, drafted apart from the shared version, reviewed in one place and approved by a person; assistants prepare, people decide."
---

A team's content goes wrong in the gaps between people. Nobody knows which version is current.
Feedback arrives in a document, a chat and two emails. The voice lives in one person's head, so
that person corrects every draft. Someone asks what is in progress and nobody can answer without
a meeting.

The method on this page closes those gaps with four rules. cckit is the tooling that keeps the
rules for you, so you can follow them by talking to Claude.

## The four rules

1. **Every piece is on one list.** Before anyone writes, the piece is recorded on the task list
   the team shares, with a title and a short description. What is not on the list is not being
   worked on.
2. **A draft is kept apart from the shared version.** The shared version of the project holds only
   what the team has approved. Each piece gets its own draft, a separate copy to work in. You can
   rewrite it ten times and the approved content does not change.
3. **A review happens in one place.** When a draft is ready, the writer asks for a review and gets
   one link. The teammate who reviews reads there, comments line by line there, and answers there.
4. **A person approves.** A review ends in "approved" or "changes needed", said by a teammate who
   did not write the piece. Claude never approves.

## How one piece moves

1. **Plan.** You tell Claude what you want to make. It adds the piece to the task list.
2. **Draft.** Claude opens a draft for that piece. You write and edit together.
3. **Review.** You ask for a review. A teammate reads it, comments, and approves it or asks for
   changes.
4. **Approved.** The approved draft becomes part of the shared version, and the piece is marked
   done.

A campaign is the same four steps for several pieces under one plan. The plan itself is reviewed
before any piece is written. See [Plan and run a campaign](/marketing/campaigns/).

## What changes for you

| Before | With this method |
| --- | --- |
| Drafts live in documents, chats and email threads. | Every piece has one draft, in one place, with its history. |
| "Which version is the latest?" | The shared version in the project is the approved one. A draft is separate until it is approved. |
| Feedback arrives in five places. | A review happens in one place, line by line, and ends in "approved" or "changes needed". |
| Nobody is sure what is in progress. | Ask Claude "where are we?" and get the list. |
| The brand voice is in someone's head. | The voice is written down in the project, and Claude reads it before it writes. |

## Assistants prepare, people decide

Claude brings four assistants to the project: a planner, an editor, a designer and a researcher.
They propose plans, write drafts, check facts and apply review comments. They do not decide what
gets made, what a draft finally says or whether a piece is approved. Those stay with your team.

The full list of people, assistants and decisions is on
[Who does what](/marketing/who-does-what/).

## What the method does not cover

- **Publishing.** The method ends when a piece is approved. Putting it on your site, sending the
  email and scheduling the post happen in the tools you already use. See
  [after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).
- **Visual identity.** The brand work here is the written voice. Logo, color and visual identity
  are outside it.
- **Results.** cckit has no analytics. A campaign plan has a place for the measured result, and
  your team supplies the number.
