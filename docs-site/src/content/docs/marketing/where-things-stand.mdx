<TutorialMeta />

Pieces pile up: a landing page waiting for a review, a post in draft, a menu page nobody has
started. You do not keep that list. You ask your assistant, and it reads the project. The example
is the Bakery site, a neighbourhood bakery's website, a few days after the landing page went to
review.

**Who does it:** you ask. Your assistant reads the project and answers. Nothing changes when you
ask.

**What you will have at the end:** you know what is yours to do next and what is waiting for
someone else.

<Steps>

1. **Ask where things stand.** The answer is short: what is in progress, what waits for a review
   and what needs cleaning up.

   <Chat
     title="asking where things stand on the Bakery site"
     say="Where are we?"
     means="A draft is yours alone. Waiting for a review means another person has to read it."
   >
     The Bakery site has one piece in draft, the Easter menu page, and one piece waiting for a
     review, the landing page. Nothing else is open and there is nothing to clean up.
   </Chat>

2. **See every piece and where it is.**

   <Chat
     title="listing every piece of the Bakery site"
     say="Show me all our pieces and where each one is."
     means="Each piece has one state. Finished pieces appear only when you ask for them. The states are listed below."
   >
     Three open pieces. Landing page: waiting for a review. Easter menu page: in draft. Post about
     the Easter loaf: not started.
   </Chat>

   <Outcomes title="All 7 states a piece can be in">

   | What you hear | What it means |
   | --- | --- |
   | `draft` | The piece is written down on this computer only. The team has not seen it. |
   | `todo` | The piece is on the shared list and nobody has started it. |
   | `shared` | The piece was sent to the team in a review that is not approved yet. |
   | `doing` | A draft for the piece has been started and is being worked on. |
   | `review` | The draft is marked done and a review is open. A person has to read it. |
   | `done` | The review was approved and the piece is part of the shared version. |
   | `dropped` | The piece was cancelled. It is listed only when you ask for finished and cancelled pieces too. |

   </Outcomes>

3. **Ask what is next.**

   <Chat
     title="asking what to hand over next on the Bakery site"
     say="What is next?"
     means="It picks by priority first, then by the day the piece was recorded."
   >
     Next is the post about the Easter loaf. It is the highest priority among the pieces nobody
     has started. Do you want me to start it?
   </Chat>

4. **Ask what is waiting for you.**

   <Chat
     title="asking what waits for a person on the Bakery site"
     say="Is anything waiting for me?"
     means="Pieces in review wait for an approver, and a draft waits for whoever wrote it."
   >
     The landing page is waiting for an approver. Nobody has read it yet. The Easter menu page is a
     draft, so it is waiting for you to ask for a review.
   </Chat>

</Steps>

<Outcomes title="For the technical person: what runs">

<div data-table="grid">

| What you say | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| "Where are we?" | `cckit status` |
| "Show me all our pieces and where each one is." | `cckit task list` |
| "Show me the finished ones too." | `cckit task list --all` |
| "What is next?" | `cckit task next` |

</div>

`cckit status` counts the light tasks by state, lists the ones somebody has to act on (`review`,
`doing`, `draft`, `shared`), and adds open reviews that wait for a person. `cckit task list` reads
the state from git and makes no GitHub call. None of the four writes anything. The verbs are in
the [CLI reference](/cli-reference/).

</Outcomes>
