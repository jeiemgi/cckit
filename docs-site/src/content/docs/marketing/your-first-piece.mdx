Writing is where most of the time goes, and where a team most fears breaking something: editing
the wrong version, or changing a page that was already signed off. So each piece gets its own
draft. You can work on it with Claude as freely as you like, and what is already approved does
not change until a teammate approves the new piece.

This page follows one blog post from the idea to the moment it is approved. Every other piece of
work, from a landing page to a product description, moves the same way.

**Who does it:** you write, with Claude and its editor assistant. A teammate approves.

**What you will have at the end:** the post as an approved file in the shared version of the
project, and the piece marked done on the task list.

**Before you start:** the project is [set up](/marketing/get-set-up/) and open in Claude Code.

<Steps>

1. **Tell Claude what you want to make.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   I want to write a blog post announcing our spring workshop. Record it as a new piece of work and start a draft for it.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude adds the piece to the task list your team shares, with a title and a short description,
   and starts a draft for it. A draft is your own copy to work in. Nothing in it changes the shared
   version until the piece is approved.

2. **Agree on an outline.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Propose an outline for this post. The reader is a small-business owner who has not heard of us. The one thing I want them to do is sign up for the workshop.
   ```

   </div>

   Change the outline until it is right. Say what to cut, what to move and what is missing.

3. **Write the draft.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Write the first draft from that outline. Follow our brand voice.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude writes the post as a file in the project and tells you where it is. Edit it together:
   "make the opening shorter", "this paragraph sounds like an ad", "add the date and the price".
   If your team has [set its brand voice](/marketing/brand-voice/), Claude reads it before it
   writes.

4. **Ask for a review.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   This is ready. Save it and ask for a review.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude saves the draft, sends it to GitHub and gives you a link. That link is the review: one
   page that shows what the post says and where teammates can comment.

5. **A teammate reviews it.** Send the link to the person who approves. On that page they open
   the **Files changed** tab, comment on any line, then click **Review changes** and choose
   **Approve** or **Request changes**. GitHub does not let the person who asked for the review
   approve it, so the approver is someone else.

6. **Apply the changes, if any.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Read the review comments on this post and apply them. Tell me which ones you did not apply and why.
   ```

   </div>

   The review page updates by itself. The reviewer looks again.

7. **Approved.** Once the review says approved:

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   The review is approved. Finish this piece.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude makes the approved draft part of the shared version, marks the piece done and tidies up
   the draft. The approver can also finish it from the review page with the green
   **Merge pull request** button (some projects label it **Squash and merge**). The piece is then
   approved and done; the draft's folder stays on your computer until you ask Claude to clean up.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Approved is not published">
cckit stops at approved. Getting the post onto your site happens the way it does today. See
[after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).
</Aside>

## Check that it worked

Ask Claude "Where are we?". The post is no longer listed as in progress or waiting for a review,
and it counts as done.

## Coming back to a piece

Drafts survive closing the app. Open the project again and say:

<div class="cckit-prompt">

```text title="Say this to Claude"
Where are we? Show me my drafts and anything waiting for a review.
```

</div>

Then say which one to continue.

## What stays with people

- **Approving.** A teammate approves on the review page. Claude finishes a piece only after you
  tell it the review is approved.
- **Publishing.** That is outside cckit.
