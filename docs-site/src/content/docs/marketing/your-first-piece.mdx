<TutorialMeta />

The first time, hand over something small and complete: one landing page, one post, one section
of a site. You describe it, your assistant builds it in the background and returns a review link,
and a person approves it. Pilot Light Bakery's project is set up and empty, and the first thing the
shop needs is a landing page.

**Who does it:** you describe the piece and read the result. Your assistant and its helpers build.
Your colleague approves; if you work alone, you finish it yourself (step 3 says how).

**What you will have at the end:** the piece as part of the shared version of the project, and the
task marked done.

**Before you start:** the [keys are given](/marketing/get-set-up/) and the project is open in the
chat with your assistant.

<Steps>

1. **Describe the piece in one paragraph.** Say what it is and what goes on it. Your assistant
   writes it down as a task and starts building:

   <Chat
     title="handing over the Pilot Light Bakery landing page"
     say="I need a landing page for my bakery, Pilot Light. At the top the name and a short line about us, then three breads with prices, our opening hours and a contact form. Can you build it?"
     means="Nothing is public yet. You will get a link, read the page and approve it or ask for changes."
   >
     I wrote it down as a task and started building. I will tell you when there is a link.
   </Chat>

   <Outcomes title="For the technical person: the two commands behind this step">

   ```text
   cckit task new "<title of the piece>" --body "<what goes on the page>"
   cckit task run <id>
   ```

   `cckit task new` prints the task's id, and `<id>` is that id. The notes after `--body` are the
   paragraph about what goes on the page.

   </Outcomes>

2. **Wait for the link.** Your assistant tells you when the page is ready and gives you one link.
   Before it does, it checks that the site still works; when that check fails, or when nothing
   changed, no review is opened and your assistant says why.

   One round of a landing page like this one took under three minutes in a real run with nobody
   stepping in; a second page, a menu, took about one minute.

3. **Look at the result.** Open the review link. [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/)
   says what the page shows. If you want something changed, say it:

   <Chat
     title="asking for changes on the Pilot Light Bakery landing page"
     say="The line at the top is too long. Use 'Bread baked this morning'. And add a photo of the shop."
     means="You only describe what to change. The review link stays the same, and nothing is approved by itself."
   >
     The line at the top is changed and the review is updated. I could not add a photo: there is
     none in the project. Send me one and I will add it.
   </Chat>

   **Who approves.** Send the link to your colleague. GitHub does not let the person who asked for
   a review approve it, so a second person reads the page and clicks **Approve**. If you work
   alone there is no second reader: you read the review yourself and finish it, either by telling
   your assistant the page is fine or with the green **Merge pull request** button on the review
   page.

4. **Approved.** Once it is approved, tell your assistant so, and it finishes the piece: the page
   becomes part of the shared version and the task is marked done. See
   [After approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).

</Steps>

{/* VERIFY-504 */}
For a page, Claude loads the design skills described on [The tools we plug in](/marketing/tools/#design-skills-how-a-page-is-designed)
and writes a short design brief before it builds. The review says which design skills were loaded
in that run.

You never edit code. Nothing is approved by itself, and cckit does not publish: the result is an
approved site that works, and putting it on a public address is a separate step.

## Check that it worked

Ask "Where are we?". The landing page is no longer listed as in progress or waiting for a review,
and it counts as done.

## Coming back to a piece

Pieces you have started are still there after you close the app. Open the project again and say:

<Chat
  title="coming back to the Bakery site the next day"
  say="Where are we? Show me what I have started and anything waiting for a review."
  means="Then say which one to continue. To pick new work instead, say: “Show me what is ready and I will pick.”"
>
  The landing page is done. You have one piece in progress, the post about the new rye loaf.
  Nothing is waiting for a review. Do you want to continue the post?
</Chat>

<Aside type="note" title="Approved is not published">
cckit stops at approved. See [after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval), and
[Put an approved piece online](/marketing/go-live/) for what to hand the person who publishes.
</Aside>
