The first time, hand over something small and complete: one landing page, one post, one section
of a site. You describe it, your assistant builds it in the background and returns a review link,
and you approve it. The example on this page is the Bakery site, a neighbourhood bakery's website.

**Who does it:** you describe and approve. Your assistant and its helpers build.

**What you will have at the end:** the piece as an approved version in the project, and the task
marked done.

**Before you start:** the [keys are given](/marketing/get-set-up/) and the project is open in the
chat with your assistant.

## Option 1: hand it over

<Steps>

1. **Describe the piece in one paragraph.** Say what it is and what goes on it. Your assistant
   records it as a task and starts building:

   <Chat
     title="handing over the bakery landing page"
     say="Record a new task: a landing page for a bakery. A hero with the name and a tagline, three products with prices, opening hours, and a contact form. Then run it."
     means="Nothing is public yet. You read the page and approve it or ask for changes."
   >
     Recorded as a task called "A landing page for a bakery", and I have started it. It runs in the
     background. I will tell you when there is a link to review.
   </Chat>

   <Outcomes title="For the technical person: the two commands behind this step">

   ```text
   cckit task new "A landing page for a bakery" --body "<what goes on the page>"
   cckit task run <id>
   ```

   `cckit task new` prints the task's id, and `<id>` is that id. The notes after `--body` are the
   paragraph about what goes on the page.

   </Outcomes>

2. **Wait for the link.**

   <Chat
     title="waiting for the bakery landing page"
     say="How is the landing page coming along?"
     means="The project's check passes before the review is opened. When the check fails, or when nothing changed, no review is opened and your assistant says why."
   >
     It is ready. The page has the four sections you asked for and the project's check passed, so I
     opened a review. Here is the link to look at it.
   </Chat>

   One unattended round of a landing page like this one took under three minutes in a real run; a
   second page, a menu, took about one minute.

3. **Look at the result and approve.** Open the review link. See
   [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/) for what the page shows. If you want
   changes, say so:

   <Chat
     title="asking for changes on the bakery landing page"
     say="Read the review comments on this task and apply them. Tell me which ones you did not apply and why."
     means="You only describe what to change. Nothing merges by itself."
   >
     I read the comments and applied the ones I could. The tagline is now the one you suggested. I
     did not apply the comment asking for a photo of the shop, because there is no photo in the
     project yet. Send me one and I will add it.
   </Chat>

4. **Approved.** Approving and merging the review marks the task done:

   <Chat
     title="finishing the bakery landing page"
     say="The review is approved. Finish this piece."
     means="Approved is not online. Putting the page on a public address is a separate step."
   >
     Done. The landing page is part of the project now and the task is marked done.
   </Chat>

</Steps>

{/* VERIFY-504 */}
For a page, Claude loads the design skills described on [The tools we plug in](/marketing/tools/#design-skills-how-a-page-is-designed)
and writes a short design brief before it builds. The review says which design skills were loaded
in that run.

You never edit code. Nothing merges by itself, and cckit does not publish: the result is an
approved site that builds, and putting it on a public address is a separate step.

## Option 2: write it together

Use this for a piece you want to shape sentence by sentence, such as a blog post. You work with
your assistant and its editor helper in a draft of your own, and the approved version does not
change until the piece is approved.

<Steps>

1. **Tell your assistant what you want to make and start the draft.**

   <Chat
     title="starting a blog post for the bakery"
     say="I want to write a blog post announcing our Easter menu. Record it as a new piece of work and start a draft for it."
     means="The draft is yours alone. The approved version of the site does not change."
   >
     Recorded as a new piece of work, and the draft is open. Tell me who should read the post and
     what you want them to do.
   </Chat>

2. **Agree on an outline.**

   <Chat
     title="agreeing on an outline for the Easter post"
     say="Propose an outline for this post. The reader is a neighbour who has not tried our bread. The one thing I want them to do is order an Easter loaf."
     means="Change the outline until it is right: what to cut, what to move, what is missing."
   >
     Here is an outline: a short opening about the Easter loaf, what goes in it, when you can
     collect it, and one line on how to order. Do you want the story of the recipe before the
     ordering line?
   </Chat>

3. **Write the draft.**

   <Chat
     title="writing the first draft of the Easter post"
     say="Write the first draft from that outline. Follow our brand voice."
     means="Edit it together: “make the opening shorter”, “this paragraph sounds like an ad”, “add the date and the price”."
   >
     The first draft is written as a file in the project, and I will tell you where it is. I wrote
     it in the brand voice your team set.
   </Chat>

   If your team has [set its brand voice](/marketing/brand-voice/), your assistant reads it first.

4. **Ask for a review.**

   <Chat
     title="asking for a review of the Easter post"
     say="This is ready. Save it and ask for a review."
     means="Your assistant saves the draft, sends it to GitHub and gives you a link."
   >
     Saved, and the review is open. Here is the link to send to the person who approves.
   </Chat>

5. **A teammate reviews it, and you apply the changes.** Send the link to the person who
   approves. GitHub does not let the person who asked for the review approve it, so the approver
   is someone else. Then:

   <Chat
     title="applying the reviewer's comments on the Easter post"
     say="Read the review comments on this post and apply them. Tell me which ones you did not apply and why."
     means="You decide what to change. Your assistant reports every comment it left alone."
   >
     I applied the comments about the opening and the price. I left one comment alone: it asks for
     a second photo, and there is none in the project. Do you want to add one?
   </Chat>

6. **Approved.** Once the review says approved:

   <Chat
     title="finishing the Easter post"
     say="The review is approved. Finish this piece."
     means="The approver can also finish it from the review page with the green Merge pull request button (some projects label it Squash and merge)."
   >
     Done. The approved draft is part of the shared version, the piece is marked done and I
     tidied up the draft.
   </Chat>

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

<Chat
  title="checking that the piece is done"
  say="Where are we?"
  means="The piece is no longer listed as in progress or waiting for a review, and it counts as done."
>
  The Easter post is done. Nothing is in progress and nothing is waiting for a review.
</Chat>

## Coming back to a piece

Drafts survive closing the app. Open the project again and say:

<Chat
  title="coming back to the bakery drafts"
  say="Where are we? Show me my drafts and anything waiting for a review."
  means="Then say which one to continue. To pick new work instead, say: “Show me what is ready and I will pick.”"
>
  You have one draft, the Easter post, and one review waiting, the landing page. Which one do you
  want to continue?
</Chat>

<Aside type="note" title="Approved is not published">
cckit stops at approved. See [after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).
</Aside>
