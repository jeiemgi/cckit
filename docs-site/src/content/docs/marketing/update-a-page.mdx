<TutorialMeta />

An approved page is finished. When the bakery changes its hours or runs out of a bread, the change
is a new piece of work with its own review, so the person who approves reads only what changed.
Pilot Light Bakery's Easter menu page was approved two weeks ago; now Good Friday is close and one
loaf has sold out.

**Who does it:** you describe the change. Your assistant builds it in the background. Your
colleague approves.

**What you will have at the end:** the updated page as part of the shared version, and the change
recorded as its own piece.

<Steps>

1. **Name the page and say what changes.** Say which page it is and list every change. Your
   assistant writes it down as a new piece of work and starts it.

   <Chat
     title="handing over an update to the Easter menu page"
     say="The Easter menu page needs two changes. Add our Good Friday opening hours, 8 to 12, and say the walnut loaf is sold out."
     means="Nothing is public. The page that was approved stays as it is until this change is approved."
   >
     I wrote it down as an update to the Easter menu page and started it. It works from the page as
     it is in the project now. I will tell you when there is a link to review.
   </Chat>

2. **Wait for the link.** As with a new piece, your assistant checks that the site still works
   before it opens a review. When that check fails, or when nothing changed, no review opens and
   your assistant says why.

3. **Read what changed.** The review shows the changes, with new text in green, and not the whole
   page. If you want it in plain words first, ask.

   <Chat
     title="asking what changed on the Easter menu page"
     say="What changed on the Easter menu page? In plain words."
     means="Check the list against the review. The review is the one that counts."
   >
     Two changes. A line was added with the Good Friday opening hours, 8 to 12. The walnut loaf is
     now marked sold out.
   </Chat>

4. **Approve and finish.** The approver reads the review as described in
   [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/). Once it is approved, tell your
   assistant so, and it finishes the piece (see
   [After approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval)).

</Steps>

A page that was not made through cckit works the same way, as long as it is part of the project:
name the page and say what changes.

<Aside type="note" title="Approved is not published">
The updated page is not online until someone puts it there. See
[Put an approved piece online](/marketing/go-live/).
</Aside>

<Outcomes title="For the technical person: the two commands behind step 1">

```text
cckit task new "Update the Easter menu page" --body "<the page and every change>"
cckit task run <id>
```

`cckit task new` prints the task's id, and `<id>` is that id. `cckit task run` builds one
objective from the task's title and notes, so the notes must name the page. The task's gate is
`--check`, else `tasks.check` in the project config. A failing gate opens no review. The earlier
task for the page is done, and `cckit task start` refuses a done task, which is why the change is
a new task.

</Outcomes>
