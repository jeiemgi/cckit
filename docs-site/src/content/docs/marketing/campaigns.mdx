title: Plan and run a campaign
description: Turn one marketing goal into a campaign plan, break it into pieces of work, and take every piece to approved.
---

import { Steps, Aside } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

A campaign is several pieces that have to tell one story and be ready together: a landing page,
three emails, five social posts. Written one by one with no plan, they drift apart, and nobody
can say what is finished. Planning them under one goal shows what is written, what is stuck and
what is left.

**Who does it:** you state the goal and approve the plan. The planner assistant proposes the
pieces and keeps the list. The pieces themselves are written afterwards, one at a time.

**What you will have at the end:** an approved campaign plan saved in the project, every piece on
the task list, and each piece taken to approved.

**Before you start:** your [brand voice](/marketing/brand-voice/) is set, because the plan takes
its audience and its message from it. You have taken
[one piece to approved](/marketing/your-first-piece/), because every piece of a campaign moves the
same way.

<Steps>

1. **State the goal.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   Plan a campaign. The goal is 200 sign-ups for our spring workshop by April 15. The audience is small-business owners in our newsletter and on Instagram.
   ```

   </div>

   One goal, one audience and one date are enough to start. Claude asks for what is missing. A
   goal needs a number and a date; a second goal or a second audience is a second campaign.

2. **Read the plan.** Claude proposes the campaign: the message, the pieces, the order and what
   each piece is for. The plan opens as a page in your browser where you can comment and answer.
   When no browser is available, it is shown in the conversation.

3. **Change it until it is right.** Remove pieces you will not make. Add the ones it missed. Nothing
   has been created yet. A plan holds at most eight pieces; more than that is two campaigns.

4. **Approve the plan.**

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   The plan is good. Create the campaign and its pieces.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude records the campaign and one piece of work for each piece in the plan. Nothing is
   recorded before you say yes. How the pieces are recorded and reviewed depends on where your
   project keeps its task list: see [Two ways a campaign is tracked](#two-ways-a-campaign-is-tracked).

5. **Work through the pieces.** You write each piece the way you wrote
   [your first one](/marketing/your-first-piece/): outline, draft, edits.

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   What is next in the campaign? Let's write it.
   ```

   </div>

6. **Ask for a review.** With the task list in the project, ask when each piece is ready, as you
   did for your first piece. With the task list on GitHub, ask once, when every piece is written:

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   The campaign is ready. Ask for the review.
   ```

   </div>

   That one review covers all the pieces, so the approver reads them side by side and can check
   that they tell one story.

7. **Approved.** With the task list in the project, finish each piece when its review is approved.
   With the task list on GitHub, once the campaign review says approved:

   <div class="cckit-prompt">

   ```text title="Say this to Claude"
   The campaign review is approved. Finish the campaign.
   ```

   </div>

   Claude makes the approved pieces part of the shared version and marks the campaign and every
   piece under it done.

</Steps>

## Two ways a campaign is tracked

The marketing setup keeps the task list in the project. The technical person can switch it to
GitHub. To find out which one yours uses, ask Claude: "Does this project keep its task list in the
project or on GitHub?"

| | Task list in the project | Task list on GitHub |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **When you approve the plan** | The plan and the list of its pieces go into one review. Once that review is approved, every piece is on the shared list as not started. | The campaign appears on GitHub with each piece under it. |
| **How the pieces are reviewed** | One at a time. Each piece has its own draft and its own review. | Together. The pieces share one draft and get one review at the end. |
| **How many at once** | No limit. | Two campaigns in progress is the default limit. The technical person can change it. |

## See where a campaign stands

<div class="cckit-prompt">

```text title="Say this to Claude"
Where are we on the campaign? What is done, what is in draft and what is waiting for someone?
```

## Good to know

- **Finish a campaign only when every piece is written.** With the task list on GitHub, finishing
  the campaign marks every piece under it done, including one nobody wrote. With the task list in
  the project, each piece is finished on its own, so an unwritten piece stays on the list.
- **Research before you plan.** Ask Claude to look at what competitors are saying, or to check a
  claim, before the plan is written. It reports its sources.

<Aside type="note">
Sending the emails and scheduling the posts are outside cckit. The campaign ends when its pieces
are approved.
</Aside>

## After the campaign

When the goal's date has passed, tell Claude the number you measured. It records it in the plan.
cckit does not measure it for you, and a campaign with no number is recorded as not measured.
