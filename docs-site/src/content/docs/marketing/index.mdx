cckit gives a marketing team one way to move every piece of work from idea to approved: a blog
post, a landing page, a campaign, a change to the brand voice. You do the work by talking to
Claude. cckit keeps track of it.

You do not need to open a terminal or learn developer tools. Someone technical sets the project up
once; after that you work in plain language.

## What changes for you

| Without cckit | With cckit |
| --- | --- |
| Drafts live in documents, chats and email threads. | Every piece has one draft, in one place, with its history. |
| "Which version is the latest?" | The shared version in the project is the approved one. A draft is separate until it is approved. |
| Feedback arrives in five places. | A review happens in one place, line by line, and ends in "approved" or "changes needed". |
| Nobody is sure what is in progress. | Ask Claude "where are we?" and get the list. |
| The brand voice is in someone's head. | The voice is written down in the project, and Claude reads it before it writes. |

## How the work moves

Every piece follows the same four steps.

1. **Plan.** You tell Claude what you want to make. It adds the piece to the task list your team shares.
2. **Draft.** Claude opens a draft for that piece. You write and edit together. Nothing you do in a
   draft changes the shared version.
3. **Review.** When the draft is ready, you ask for a review. A teammate reads it, comments, and
   approves it or asks for changes.
4. **Approved.** The approved draft becomes part of the shared version, and the piece is marked
   done.

A campaign is the same four steps for several pieces at once, under one plan.

<Aside type="note" title="What cckit does not do">
cckit takes a piece to **approved**. It does not create a website and it does not publish. How
approved content reaches your site, your newsletter tool or your social accounts stays the way it
is today.
</Aside>
