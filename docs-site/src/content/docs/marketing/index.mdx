You hand over a piece of work: a content-driven page, a landing page, a small app, a website, a
post. You describe what you want in plain words. cckit builds it in the background and gives you
back a link to look at. You read it, and you approve it or ask for changes.

The steps in between (drafts, checks, review) happen behind the scenes. You do not run them and
you do not need to learn them. [What happens after you hand it over](/marketing/how-we-work/)
tells them briefly, so you know what you are trusting.

## Where you act

1. **Give the keys, once.** A technical person sets up the accounts and the project one time.
   [Give the keys](/marketing/get-set-up/)
2. **Say what you want.** A paragraph is enough: what the page or the piece is, what goes on it.
   [Hand over your first piece](/marketing/your-first-piece/)
3. **Read what came back.** A handed-over piece is built without stopping to ask. Where your
   paragraph could be read two ways your assistant picks one, and you see which in the review.
4. **Approve.** You open the link, read, and approve or ask for changes. A person decides, never
   your assistant. [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/)

## What you get

A link to a version of the work you can look at, and an approved piece once a person says yes. The
project remembers your brand voice and, with the optional memory turned on, the decisions you made
in earlier conversations. See [The tools we plug in](/marketing/tools/).

## After the first piece

- [Write a piece together](/marketing/write-together/): shape a post sentence by sentence.
- [Ask for changes to a piece](/marketing/ask-for-changes/): change a piece that is being written or in review.
- [Update a page that already exists](/marketing/update-a-page/): a change to a piece that was approved.
- [Put an approved piece online](/marketing/go-live/): what to hand the person who publishes.
- [Several pieces, one goal](/marketing/campaigns/): a campaign planned together and approved piece by piece.
- [See where everything stands](/marketing/where-things-stand/): pieces being written, pieces
  waiting for a review, pieces done.

## What cckit does not do

- **It does not publish.** The result is an approved site that works. Putting it on a public
  address is a separate step, done by the technical person or by your hosting service.
- **It does not approve for you.** Nothing is approved by itself.
- **It does not skip the setup.** Someone technical sets up the keys once, and the first setup
  takes a terminal.
