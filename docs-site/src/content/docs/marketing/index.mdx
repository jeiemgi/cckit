You hand over a piece of work: a content-driven page, a landing page, a small app, a website, a
post. You describe what you want in plain words. cckit builds it in the background and gives you
back a link to look at. You read it, and you approve it or ask for changes.

The steps in between (drafts, checks, review) happen behind the scenes. You do not run them and
you do not need to learn them. [What happens after you hand it over](/marketing/how-we-work/)
tells them briefly, so you know what you are trusting.

## Where you act

1. **Give the keys, once.** A technical person sets up the accounts and the project one time.
   [Give the keys](/marketing/get-set-up/)
2. **Say what you want.** A paragraph is enough: what the page or the piece is, what goes on it.
   [Hand over your first piece](/marketing/your-first-piece/)
3. **Read what came back.** A handed-over task runs without stopping to ask. Where your paragraph
   could be read two ways it picks one, and you see which in the review.
4. **Approve.** You open the link, read, and approve or ask for changes. A person decides, never
   Claude. [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/)

## What you get

A link to a version of the work you can look at, and an approved version once you say yes. The
project remembers your brand voice and, with the optional memory turned on, the decisions you made
in earlier sessions. See [The tools we plug in](/marketing/tools/).

## What cckit does not do

- **It does not publish.** The result is an approved site that builds. Putting it on a public
  address is a separate step, done by the technical person or by your hosting service.
- **It does not approve for you.** Nothing is merged by itself in this flow.
- **It does not skip the setup.** Someone technical sets up the keys once, and the first setup
  takes a terminal.

<Aside type="note" title="Proved on a real run">
One command took a task written as one paragraph, "a landing page for a bakery" with a hero,
three products with prices, opening hours and a contact form, and returned a review link. One
unattended round took under three minutes, and the project's check passed before the review was
opened. The person did not edit code.
</Aside>
