A marketing team rarely loses time on the writing itself. It loses time around it: finding the
latest version, collecting feedback from five places, explaining the brand voice again, asking who
is working on what.

cckit is a way of working that removes those questions. Every piece, from a blog post to a
campaign, moves the same way from idea to approved. You do the work by talking to Claude. cckit
keeps the record of what is planned, what is in draft, what is waiting for a review and what is
approved.

You do not open a terminal or learn developer tools. Someone technical sets the project up once;
after that you work in plain language.

## The process in five steps

1. **Set up.** A technical person prepares the project once and connects the few tools the method
   uses. You check that it worked by asking Claude one question.
   [Get set up](/marketing/get-set-up/) · [The tools we plug in](/marketing/tools/)
2. **Set the voice.** The team writes down how the brand sounds, what it always says and what it
   never says. Claude reads that before it writes anything.
   [Set your brand voice](/marketing/brand-voice/)
3. **Plan.** You state a goal. Claude proposes the pieces that serve it, you correct the plan, and
   the pieces go on the task list the team shares.
   [Plan and run a campaign](/marketing/campaigns/)
4. **Create.** Each piece gets its own draft. You write and edit it with Claude, and nothing in a
   draft changes what is already approved.
   [A piece, idea to approved](/marketing/your-first-piece/)
5. **Review and approve.** A teammate reads the draft in one place, comments, and approves it or
   asks for changes. A person decides, never Claude.
   [Review and approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/)

Steps 1 and 2 happen once. Steps 3 to 5 repeat for every piece. A single piece with no campaign
skips step 3: you tell Claude what you want to make and start at step 4.

<Aside type="note" title="Where the process ends">
cckit takes a piece to **approved**. It does not create a website and it does not publish. How
approved content reaches your site, your newsletter tool or your social accounts stays the way it
is today. See [what happens after approval](/marketing/review-and-approval/#after-approval).
</Aside>
