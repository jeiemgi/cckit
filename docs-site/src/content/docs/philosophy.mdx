<p class="human-note">✍️ The only page in these docs a human wrote — not an AI.</p>

Over years of building software, the actions cckit automates are the ones I've done again and again.
They *are* the development process.

With the surge of "vibe coders" — people arriving from every background, technical and not — the same
problems keep resurfacing, more and more often. cckit is my small answer to that: a basic setup, a
toolbelt I put together while reading about and documenting the process of shipping the very first
product of my own.

I wanted to share it — with people who've done this for a while, and especially with people just
joining the software-development community. **Develop software with care.** If this gets even a few
more people interested in *how* to build software well, it did its job.

So think of it as a guide. You pick up some of the old-school discipline — SCRUM, issue tracking — and
carry it into a new era, where you can forget the mechanics and become the **idea generator, the
architect**. The structural engineering — the part that makes the thing actually stand up — is what
cckit takes care of.

## If you're a vibe coder

Welcome to this world. Be curious, and be kind — that's how the open-source community works, and it's
how you'll get the most out of it. I've built in sections with **ready-to-copy prompts** and
plain-language explanations pitched at **Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced** levels, so you can start
exactly where you are.

## If you already know the mechanics

This should flow for you. Along the way it tries to introduce ideas that are becoming more and more
relevant — like **parallel agentic development** — without getting in your way.

## The concept, in one line

> **Turn your Git into retrievable, efficient context** — using issues, GitHub Projects, and other
> people's tools, wired together with a few advanced mechanics.

That's it. You bring the ideas. cckit keeps the memory and runs the machinery.
